# ADR-008: Use PostgreSQL as the Source of Truth

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** 2026-08-21

## Context

The platform ingests structured and unstructured financial data from APIs, PDFs, HTML pages, CSVs, Excel files, filings, and transcripts. Before semantic enrichment, the system requires a single authoritative store for normalized business data with transactional consistency and traceability.

## Decision

Use **PostgreSQL** as the system of record for all normalized market and business data.

- Market Data Service parses, validates, and normalizes incoming data before writing to PostgreSQL.
- Each record includes metadata such as `content_type`, `source`, `company_id`, `document_id`, and `published_at`.
- Knowledge Synchronization reads normalized records from PostgreSQL before generating embeddings and GraphRAG artifacts.
- PostgreSQL stores structured business data only; semantic retrieval is handled by pgvector and GraphRAG.

## Consequences

### Advantages

- Single authoritative source for normalized data.
- ACID transactions ensure data integrity.
- Strong support for structured financial records and metadata.
- Clear separation between operational and semantic storage.

### Trade-offs

- Requires downstream synchronization for semantic indexes.
- Does not provide vector search or graph traversal capabilities.

## Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | Reason Rejected |
|------------|-----------------|
| Storing raw data directly in vector storage | Loses structured source-of-truth semantics. |
| Graph-only storage | Poor fit for transactional financial data. |
| NoSQL-first approach | Weaker fit for relational financial records and consistency requirements. |
